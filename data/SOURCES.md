# Why this problem is large (industry numbers, not our results)

These figures describe clinical trials as they are run today. ENROLBOUND is a synthetic demo. We have not measured a saving. Do not quote these as product outcomes. Do not quote TrialPulse “94% faster” or “↓40% deviations.”

## Protocol deviations are common, and oncology is worse

Tufts CSDD working group, 187 protocols: Phase II / III mean **75 / 119 protocol deviations**; about **one third** of enrolled patients were involved. **Oncology was the worst**, with deviations affecting **>40%** of patients on those trials. More eligibility criteria and more sites raised the odds of a high-deviation protocol.

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8979478/

## Protocols keep getting heavier

Tufts / trade summaries 2024–25: Phase III protocols now run **~3.5 amendments**. One analysis put average Phase III deviations near **296** (up sharply versus a decade earlier).

https://clinicalresearch.io/blog/the-rising-complexity-of-study-design-what-it-means-for-clinical-research-sites/

## A bad enrol is how days and amendments start

Tufts CSDD 2024: mean direct cost to run a Phase II/III trial on the order of **~$40,000 per day**; Phase III daily cost is often quoted around **$55,000**. A prevented bad enrol does not “save $55k.” A deviation discovered at monitoring is how extra days and amendments start.

## Screen failures are already expensive

Screen failures commonly **20–30%+** in solid-tumour work; biomarker gates go higher. Enrolling someone with eGFR 58 when the protocol says ≥60 is exactly that waste.

## The site job is memory, not re-reading 200 pages

A coordinator holds many trials in memory. Principal investigators will not re-read 200+ pages between rooms. That is the job. (TrialPulse states this problem; we do not copy their product.)

## Honest line for the page

These numbers describe the industry. This demo is synthetic. We have not measured a saving.
